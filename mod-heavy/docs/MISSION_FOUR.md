## Learning objectives

- Detect a covert channel hidden in a **trusted, everyday artifact** (a cute animated-GIF email signature) by **file shape, not vibes**: data appended after the GIF terminator (`3B`), a file far larger than its pixels with a high-entropy tail, an over-stuffed comment extension, and an invisible 1×1 beacon frame.
- Recognise a **remote-loaded image as a beacon**: a signature `<img>` pointing at an external host instead of embedding (`cid:`), with **per-recipient tokens** that both surveil opens and carry a covert uplink.
- Spot a **look-alike of your own CDN** (`cdn-fastparcel.example` vs the real `assets.fastparcel-cdn.example`) and tie it to earlier infrastructure via a **shared nameserver** (`198.51.100.53`, the Exfil Whisper NS).
- Read the suspect artifact against a **known-good baseline** (a sister marketing mailbox's clean, `cid:`-embedded airplane GIF).
- Triage this as an **account takeover, not a malicious insider**: a foreign sign-in, a legacy app password with no MFA, and a template edit timed to the employee's PTO. **Don't burn the victim.**
- Scope two dimensions: **account/vector** (fleet signature hunt) and **how much left** (the bounded payload the image can hold, named by DLP, plus recipient-open telemetry).
- Treat exfiltration as a **data-breach case**: scope the exposed records (price list + customer PII) and engage the data-protection owner.
- Contain proportionally: cut the beacon at the host, the URL, and the remote-load path; secure the mailbox; remove the weaponised template and purge sent copies; preserve evidence first; scope + notify; hunt the fleet — **without blacking out email or touching attacker infrastructure.**

## Story

The night after Exfil Whisper, brand-protection flagged something small: the beloved animated **airplane GIF** in marketing coordinator **Tomas Okafor**'s email signature (`t.okafor@northglass.example`) no longer loads from Northglass' own CDN. It loads from a **look-alike host** — `cdn-fastparcel.example` (`203.0.113.90`, first seen 24 Sep, NS `198.51.100.53`) — and the file is **1.84 MB** where a normal airplane sig is ~176 KB.

During the quiet window after PRINT-07 was contained, the operator logged into Okafor's mailbox from `203.0.113.90` (**24 Sep 21:36Z**, while Tomas was on PTO) using a **legacy app password with no MFA** reused from an old breach list, and edited his signature template (**24 Sep 21:40Z** from a new OAuth app token). The weaponised `okafor.gif`:

1. Renders a perfectly normal 12-frame airplane (frames 1–11, ~190 KB of pixels).
2. Carries **1.58 MB appended after the `3B` GIF terminator** (entropy ≈ 7.98/8.0 — encrypted/compressed) and **64 KB of base64 in a GIF comment extension** (`lc1:…`). DLP decodes these to Northglass' **Q4 price list** (cost/margin) and **~1,400 customer contact records**.
3. Adds an invisible **1×1, 0 ms frame 12** so every render forces the remote fetch.
4. Loads **remotely** from `cdn-fastparcel.example/sig/okafor.gif?e=<token>` — a unique **per-recipient token** on each open. 310 unique tokens over 4 days = 310 confirmed opens phoned home, across **37 external press recipients × 2 sends**.

Because the GIF is **hosted on the attacker's own CDN**, the embedded ~1.6 MB batch is effectively **in their hands**. It's bounded — one quarter's price list plus a customer slice, not the whole CRM — but under 2 MB of pricing + PII is a **reportable breach**, and the signature is a **live beacon** on every already-sent copy.

**Separate infrastructure / ARG:** like Exfil Whisper, this never touched `203.0.113.77`. `cdn-fastparcel.example` shares nameserver `198.51.100.53` and registrant **"LNTRN Holdings"** with the M3 exfil domain. The pass-gated lore crumb shows the LC infrastructure map: `cdn-telemetry-sync.example` (M3, sinkholed), `cdn-fastparcel.example` (M4, sinkholed), and **`lantern-court.example`** — a third zone that **started answering 28 Sep**. The `?e=` tokens decode to a "court roster," one entry per Northglass incident (M0–M4) marked *observed* — continuing the **LNTRN / "lantern"** thread (M1 `X-Mailer: LNTRN Bulk 7`, M2 uploader `lntrn`, M3 `LNTRN Holdings`) and teasing **Mission Five · Last Light** (attribution / getting ahead of the operator).

## Answer key

### Detect (30)
Suspicious indicators (3 pts each): data appended after the GIF terminator, size & entropy anomaly, stuffed comment extension, invisible 1×1 beacon frame, remotely-loaded signature image, unique per-recipient tokens, look-alike CDN host, external sends carrying the weaponised signature, classified data in the decoded payload (DLP).
Not indicators (1 pt each for calling them correctly): the sister airplane signature (`alvarez.gif`), the newsletter open pixel, the legit 14 MB campaign deck.
Players must judge at least 6 of 12 items to move on; unjudged items score 0.

### Decide (20)
- Classification (5): **Covert-channel data exfiltration via a weaponised signature GIF (steganography + remote beacon)** 5 · account takeover / BEC 1 · malware attachment / malicious image 1 · false positive (normal signature / newsletter tracking) 0
- Justification (6): +1 per real indicator (appended-after-terminator, size/entropy, comment-block base64, remote image + per-recipient tokens, look-alike CDN as sole source, DLP price-list + customer match), −1 per non-indicator ("has an image", "is animated", "there's a newsletter tracking pixel"), clamped 0–6
- Scope · account (5): **"t.okafor's mailbox was compromised (account takeover); treat Tomas as a victim; fleet clean"** 5 · "Tomas is a malicious insider" 1 · "all of marketing's signatures compromised" 1 · "no account issue, just a misconfig" 0
- Scope · data (4): **"One bounded ~1.6 MB batch (Q4 price list + ~1,400 customer contacts) confirmed in attacker hands, plus 310 open-telemetry callbacks; treat that batch as breached and the signature a live beacon"** 4 · "entire customer database gone" 1 · "all of Northglass" 1 · "nothing left, just an image loading" 0
  Key evidence: 1.58 MB appended + 64 KB comment block; DLP named the contents; GIF hosted on the attacker's own CDN; 310 unique `?e=` tokens over 37×2 external sends.

The second scope question appears once the first is answered.

### Contain (25, clamped at 0)
Required: **block `cdn-fastparcel.example` / `203.0.113.90` + strip remote images + quarantine messages carrying the signature 5** · secure t.okafor's mailbox (reset, revoke sessions + legacy app password + rogue OAuth token, enforce MFA) 4 · remove the weaponised signature template + purge/recall the sent copies 4 · preserve the GIF, headers, and fetch logs before changing anything 4 · scope the exposed data + notify the data-protection / data owners 4 · hunt the fleet for the host, remote-load signatures, and the foothold date 4.
Neutral (0): report `cdn-fastparcel.example` to the CDN/registrar for takedown.
Penalties — overreaction: block all inline/remote images company-wide −3 · disable the whole marketing team's email −3 · reimage every marketing workstation −2. Harmful: forward/open the live beacon from the SOC −2 (fires another token, touches attacker infra) · fetch the look-alike URL from the SOC −2 · flood/"poison" the beacon host −5 (offensive act + ROE violation). Underreaction: delete the one email and close −3.

## Discussion prompts (workshops)

- Why is a cute, trusted signature GIF a near-perfect covert channel, and how would you have caught it without the brand-protection alert?
- What makes a remote-loaded image a beacon, and which controls (strip/proxy remote images, embed via `cid:`) actually close it?
- "Partial exfil" of a bounded batch — is that still a reportable breach? Who owns that decision?
- The account was taken over, not misused by Tomas. How do you investigate and communicate that without burning the employee?
- The first-C2 block (M2) and the DNS sinkhole (M3) didn't touch this — it ran on separate, pre-staged infrastructure sharing one nameserver. What does that tell you about declaring victory on a single block?
- What's the proportional containment here, and why is "block all images in email" the wrong move?
