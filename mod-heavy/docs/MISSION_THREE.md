## Learning objectives

- Detect a covert channel by **shape, not volume**: high-entropy/one-time subdomain labels, a single freshly-seen destination domain, an abnormal query type (TXT) from a host that should only ask for A records, and a fixed low-and-slow cadence tied to the quiet hours.
- Read DNS against a **known-good baseline** (a sister host with the same image and role) to separate exfil from legitimate chatty traffic (a CDN).
- Spot host-side evidence: a hidden staging directory of fixed-size base32 chunks with a progress cursor, a staged copy of the data being stolen, and a scheduled task running an inventory-unknown binary.
- Recognise an **egress policy gap**: a warehouse host talking DNS straight to an external nameserver, bypassing the internal resolver.
- Scope two dimensions: **host/data in scope** (fleet DNS hunt) and **how much left** (bytes-per-query × queries/night × nights, bounded by the `.cursor`).
- Treat exfiltration as a **data-breach case**, not just a malware case: scope the exposed records and engage the data-protection owner.
- Contain proportionally: cut the channel at the domain, the destination, and the resolver; isolate and image; remove the implant + persistence; rotate the service account and revoke standing access; hunt the fleet — without blacking out the warehouse or touching attacker infrastructure.

## Story

The night you contained Mission Two, the netflow team's overnight baseline flagged the **warehouse VLAN** (`10.20.9.0/24`): a steady trickle of DNS lookups to names no human would type. It traces to **NG-WH-PRINT-07** (`10.20.9.18`), a Linux label-printer controller that nobody manages and that isn't in the EDR rollout — but which mounts the **billing-exports fileshare** (`\\10.20.6.60\billing-exports`) read-only so it can render invoices.

During the Mission Two compromise window, the attacker logged in to PRINT-07 from NG-BLD-02 as `svc-print` (**21 Sep 20:09Z**, a password reused across label hosts) and dropped a small tunneller (hidden binary `labelcache`, created **20:11Z** — half an hour after the NG-BLD-02 implant installed persistence). Mission Two's fleet hunt couldn't see it: PRINT-07 had no EDR. It:

1. Staged `invoices_export_2026Q3.tar.gz` (46 MB) and `ap_vendor_master.csv` (9.4 MB) from the fileshare into `~/.cache/.spool/.sync/stage/` (22 Sep 00:58Z).
2. Chunked the data into fixed ~180 B base32 files in `out/` (`q000001.b32`, `q000002.b32`, …) with a `.cursor` tracking progress.
3. On a per-minute timer **only from 01:00 to 03:59** (`* 1-3 * * *` + `labelcache.timer`), sent a burst of ~16 chunks as high-entropy DNS labels under **`sync.cdn-telemetry-sync.example`** plus one numbered TXT poll (`ctl.pNNNN…`) for tasking — ≈3,060 lookups a night. Queries go through the internal resolver (`10.20.6.53`) and also **direct to the external nameserver `198.51.100.53`** (the warehouse VLAN allows outbound UDP/53 — a policy gap).

Over three nights (22–24 Sep) the channel moved **≈ 0.95 MB** (~110 B usable × ~2,880 data queries/night × 3) — under 2% of the 55 MB staged; the `.cursor` sits at chunk 8,640 of ~500,000, so most of the archive **had not left yet** when it was caught. Under 1 MB is still hundreds of billing/vendor records: partial exfil is a breach.

**Separate infrastructure / ARG:** the exfil **never used `203.0.113.77`** — it ran on separate infrastructure (a look-alike domain dressed up as boring "cdn / telemetry / sync" plumbing, NS `198.51.100.53`) from day one, so Mission Two's IP block did nothing to it. Lesson: blocking the first C2 isn't victory. The domain's whois (lore crumb, pass-gated) shows it was registered **before** the Lumen-7 implant ran, registrant org `LNTRN Holdings`, and its nameserver is authoritative for a second zone: `lantern-court.example`. This continues the **LNTRN / "lantern"** thread (M1 `X-Mailer: LNTRN Bulk 7`, M2 model uploader `lntrn`) and teases **Mission Four · Lantern Court** (attribution / after-action).

## Answer key

### Detect (30)
Suspicious indicators (3 pts each): high-entropy query names, unusual TXT-record queries, the look-alike parent domain (`cdn-telemetry-sync.example`), the scheduled exfil task, the hidden exfil binary (`labelcache`), the chunked staging files (`out/q*.b32` + `.cursor`), the staged billing exports, DNS bypassing the internal resolver (`UDP/53 → 198.51.100.53`), the low-and-slow cadence.
Not indicators (1 pt each for calling them correctly): CDN asset lookups, the nightly internal label backup, the live `/var/spool/labels` spool.
Players must judge at least 6 of 12 items to move on; unjudged items score 0.

### Decide (20)
- Classification (5): **Covert data exfiltration over a DNS tunnel (low-and-slow)** 5 · misconfig/DNS cache 1 · insider/USB 1 · false positive (CDN/telemetry) 0
- Justification (6): +1 per real indicator (entropy, TXT series, single look-alike domain, cadence, staging dir + archive, per-minute hidden task), −1 per non-indicator (query volume alone, "busy host", nightly backup), clamped 0–6
- Scope · host (5): **"NG-WH-PRINT-07 only; abused read access to billing-exports; fleet clean"** 5 · "the fileshare itself is compromised" 1 · "every warehouse host" 1 · "nothing" 0
- Scope · data (4): **"Partial exfil: ~0.95 MB (under 2% of 55 MB staged) left over three nights; rest staged; treat what left as breached and the staged data as at risk"** 4 · "entire archive gone" 1 · "all of Northglass" 1 · "nothing left, only lookups" 0
  Key evidence: staged 55 MB; ~110 B usable per query × ~2,880 data queries/night × 3 nights ≈ 0.95 MB; `.cursor` at chunk 8,640 of ~500,000.

The second scope question appears once the first is answered.

### Contain (25, clamped at 0)
Required: **sinkhole `cdn-telemetry-sync.example` + block `198.51.100.53` + pin the warehouse VLAN to the internal resolver 5** · isolate NG-WH-PRINT-07 (keep on, image) 4 · remove `labelcache` + its timer/cron persistence (after the image) 4 · rotate the reused `svc-print` password on every label host + revoke PRINT-07's billing-exports access 4 · scope the exposed data and notify the billing / data-protection owners 4 · fleet hunt for the domain/cadence/staging dir/foothold date 4.
Neutral (0): report the look-alike domain to the DNS provider for takedown.
Penalties — overreaction: block all outbound DNS company-wide −3 · shut down all label printing / warehouse systems −3 · reimage every warehouse host −2. Harmful: reimage NG-WH-PRINT-07 before capture −2 · resolve the exfil domain from the SOC −2 (touches attacker infra) · flood/"poison" `198.51.100.53` −5 (offensive act + ROE violation). Underreaction: flush the resolver cache + clear staging files and close −3.

### Document (25)
Keyword-matched (case-insensitive; `[.]`, `(.)`, `[@]`, `hxxp` normalised before matching):
- Summary (6): DNS-tunnel/covert exfil 2 · NG-WH-PRINT-07 (overlooked appliance) 2 · exposed billing/invoice/vendor data 2
- IOCs (9): `cdn-telemetry-sync.example` 2 · `198.51.100.53` 2 · `~/.cache/.spool/.sync/` path 2 · `labelcache` binary 1 · `labelcache.timer` / per-minute cron 1 · host `NG-WH-PRINT-07` 1
- Actions (5, 6 keys capped at 5): channel cut (sinkhole/block/resolver pinning) · host isolated · tunneller + persistence removed · `svc-print` rotated / fileshare access revoked · breach scoped + data owner notified · fleet hunt
- Lessons learned (5, 6 keys capped at 5): DNS monitoring/analytics (entropy, newly-seen domains, NXDOMAIN) · egress control (force internal resolver, block direct port 53) · least privilege for appliances & fileshare access · asset inventory + EDR coverage for overlooked devices · data controls on exports (DLP, don't stage raw exports on edge hosts) · post-incident threat hunt for persistence

Keyword scoring is a coaching aid, not a grader of prose. For team workshops, have a lead review the report text too.

## Discussion prompts (workshops)

- Why is a label printer a good place to hide an exfil channel, and how would you have found it without the netflow alert?
- What makes DNS a favourite covert channel, and which controls (internal-resolver pinning, egress port blocks, DNS analytics) actually close it?
- "Partial exfil" — is that still a reportable breach? Who owns that decision, and what do they need from you?
- Why is "block all DNS" the wrong move, and what's the proportional version?
- Mission Two's IP block didn't touch this channel — it ran on separate infrastructure from day one. What does that tell you about declaring victory on a single block?
- Mission Two's fleet hunt came back clean, yet PRINT-07 was compromised the same evening. What did the hunt not cover (EDR coverage), and how do you find the devices you don't know you have?
